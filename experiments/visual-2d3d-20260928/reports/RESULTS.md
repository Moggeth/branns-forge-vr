# Orchard Brook experiment: results

**Recommendation:** Use the saved **plan → validation → Godot compiler** boundary for future environments, and commission specialist 3D assets selectively. The generated-image plan was genuinely extracted and can be edited and rebuilt, but it did not produce the best-looking player-height scene by itself. Direct Opus A has the most inviting, varied approach in the matched captures. The hybrid E has the clearest specialist bridge and beacon and the best repeatable cross-tool workflow, yet it inherits B's open, straight approach. Keep A as the current walkable visual reference; use E as the production-method prototype, with route/terrain composition as its next correction. [Walk A](../../../godot/experiments/visual_2d3d/preview/README.md) · [walk E](../../../godot/experiments/visual_2d3d/preview/README.md) · [compare all five](../gallery/index.html).

| Method | What the experiment established | Primary limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A · written brief → Opus 3D | Lush framing, route-side variety and stronger player-height composition without 2D planning. Editable Blender scene and imported GLB. | More specialist time; the shared compiler omits some authored beacon detail and its path tiles read as slabs. Geometry is about 733k triangles before LOD. |
| B · generated image → extracted plan → Godot | The original map pixels determined stream, path, hills, bridge, clearing and 113 placements. Registered masks, corrections and JSON reproduce the layout without reinterpreting the image. | Extracted dimensions and marker positions needed explicit repair; the approach is sparse despite a valid route. Height/density layers are derived estimates, not independent art. |
| C · generated references → Opus 3D | The board led to a coherent stone bridge, bank rocks and beacon identity, with an editable, validated scene. | The map and board disagreed about bridge angle and approach; C required interpretation and the longest specialist commission. Its roughly 957k-triangle full-LOD scene is unproven in VR. |
| D · authored deterministic rules | Fastest to re-seed and alter locally; three seeds and a clearing edit passed evaluation. The current common library can make a pleasant coherent corridor without image generation. | Generic composition and limited material character. This is a strong engineering control, not a counterfeit image-led result. |
| E · B layout + constrained C props + Opus bridge/beacon | Rebuildable hybrid with 191 placements, imported specialist visuals, valid deck/landmark raycasts and all five matched renders. Added props and assets make the crossing and goal more distinctive. | It required more total work than any single baseline. The opening view remains close to B, so the hybrid hypothesis has not earned a blanket visual-quality win. |

The five [fixed-camera Godot captures](../gallery/index.html) are the fair cross-method comparison: same common orchard library, renderer and camera definitions. Separate Blender renders document the authored A/C scenes and should not be visually scored as if they used identical lighting. The gallery can hide method labels before the first review. By eye, A wins route composition, C adds designed architectural rhythm, D is clear but generic, and B/E reveal the weakness of the extracted long straight approach. This is a qualitative judgement, not a blinded human panel score.

## Evidence that changed the result

- The generated [1254 px source map](../planning/input/generated-map-source.png) was resized and classified into one registered 512 px stack. The [B preview](../planning/artifacts/B/plan_preview.png) is rendered back **from the JSON**, so missing/altered regions are visible. Its bridge paint split the stream into two detected components; the extractor joins them. Image markers were 2.7619 m and 5.9421 m from the fixed spawn/goal and were snapped while retaining the interior route. All 113 object heights now sample the declared terrain. These corrections made B evaluable without pretending the raw image was a complete specification.
- A initially failed hard clearance checks because a circular bridge exclusion rejected valid nearby props; an oriented bridge zone and one moved cliff resolved it. C needed explicit Gaussian terrain and stream-carve semantics, a narrower bank-boulder exception and two intentional outcrop embeds. These are saved corrections to representation, not new generated scenes.
- E's [targeted correction](../variants/E/correction.md) kept B's layout and 113 objects, added 78 clearance-checked C placements, and substituted a real [reference-assisted bridge](../asset_trial/with_ref/review_three_quarter.png) and C beacon. The crossing improved visibly; the sparse approach did not. The saved E builder and hashes make this comparison repeatable.
- The procedural D clearing edit increased its radius from 6 to 9 m, removed only two interfering trees, left 55 other objects and the route unchanged, and passed before/after evaluation. See [edit metrics](../evaluation/edit-metrics.json). Two further D seeds are saved; they exercise variation without another model call.

## Individual asset trial

Opus 5.5 built two editable bridges in serial. [Without a reference](../asset_trial/no_ref/review_three_quarter.png), it made an airy timber-braced span with stone posts (9,152 visual triangles, nine box colliders). [With a generated model sheet](../asset_trial/with_ref/review_three_quarter.png), it made a stone-arched, timber-deck bridge with parapets and moss (9,832 visual triangles, three colliders). Both have a 3.4 m clear walkway and survived independent reimport. Side/top/underside views are saved in each trial directory. The reference helped specify material identity and a mechanically readable arch; the no-reference model is also coherent and lighter. This single trial cannot establish a general quality or efficiency advantage. The reference sheet had view contradictions, logged in [REFERENCE-CHECK.md](../references/REFERENCE-CHECK.md), so dimensions and top view took precedence. Both assets use **flat material colours without UV textures**: generated reference imagery improved form, not texture quality. E uses the reference-assisted bridge with a simpler Godot safety deck collider.

## Secondary probes and scale

The [foliage card](../probes/renders/foliage_front.png) retains attractive 2D detail at one angle, but the [75° view](../probes/renders/foliage_side_75deg.png) exposes its crossed planes, and a 64 mm stereo-offset pair is only a flat-image proxy for headset parallax. A [view-matched panel](../probes/renders/view_matched.png) loses depth and a traversable route [70° off-axis](../probes/renders/view_unfamiliar_70deg.png). Use image-derived cards for distant dressing; keep grabbable trees and traversable structures in 3D. Texture extraction left visible background residue, and no seam-free tileable material was established.

The deterministic [160 × 160 m E scale plan](../variants/scale/world_spec.json) contains 572 placements, 386 of them added beyond the core. Five inherited props blocked the extended route on the first run and were explicitly removed, with identities saved in `scale_probe.route_cleared`. The corrected Godot build produced [overhead](../evidence/scale/overhead.png), [approach](../evidence/scale/approach.png) and [crossing](../evidence/scale/crossing.png) captures. Its 120 physics route samples had zero missing or blocking hits, three deck hits and a maximum adjacent height step of 0.385 m. The render exposes repeated trees, a long uniform path, abrupt world edges and a stream that visually stops at the boundary. These are meaningful scaling weaknesses despite passing collision checks.

## Measured cost and limits

The exact scale build on this PC took **4.155 s process wall time**, of which the Godot metrics recorded **2.099 s scene build** and **0.185 s five-view render/readback**. It had 51,200 terrain triangles, 3,780 scene nodes and 572 explicit assets. The five 80 m common-compiler builds recorded approximately **0.38–0.77 s build** and **0.12–0.25 s render** in their frozen metrics; compare these as short desktop construction/readback measurements, not sustained frame times. A and C specialist GLBs are much heavier than the shared proxy scenes. No headset frame timing, stereo comfort, full player traversal, streaming cost, or memory peak has been measured. The [structured evaluator](../evaluation/metrics.csv) reports zero hard failures for A–E but does not know full mesh radii for A/B/C, and C's shoreline contact remains unverified. A true benchmark would additionally measure GPU frame time on the Vive Pro 2 with close-view LOD and collision enabled.

Measured serial Opus commission wall times were A **399.929 s**, C **505.115 s**, bridge without reference **176.910 s**, bridge with reference **270.580 s**. The three genuine generated-image calls took **40.2**, **48.7** and **55.6 s**. Opus was verified in the cleaned Claude child environment under the personal `claude.ai` subscription account with actual session model `claude-opus-5-5`; no API-billed substitute was used. Parallel GPT-6 Sol agents implemented the extraction, evaluator, Godot compiler, walkable preview, 2.5D probes and gallery. Model and step details are in [timing-ledger.csv](timing-ledger.csv) and each component's timing file. E includes the time of B, C, the bridge trial and integration; it is **not** an equal-budget win.

## Next development step

1. Re-compose E's path and terrain directly in its saved plan: a gently curved player-height approach, partial orchard canopy and two meaningful clearings. Keep its source-image layer and explicit edit provenance, then run the same five cameras plus an unfamiliar player traverse.
2. Replace common flat terrain/path presentation with a small tested material palette and detail decals; create actual UV/texture trials for the bridge and nearby ground, rather than assuming 2D references became materials.
3. Measure the chosen scene in the headset, with asset LOD, stereo parallax, collisions and GPU frame-time logging before moving any part into the live orchard.

This work is saved locally and was not published or merged into the live game.
