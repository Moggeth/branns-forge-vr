# Stone foundation tiles

Build Stone Foundation in Menu > Manage the forge > Build, or the VR workshop. Cost: 8 Stone; health: 400. Place a machine on the same tile afterwards. The passive tile needs no Use action; damage can be repaired from Built.

Tiles cover a six-metre square and snap to the existing 0.75 m terrain grid. The displayed square preview matches that grid. Placement records the support elevation permanently; the terrain mesh, authority movement and visual sampling use the same anchored plane while the tile is alive. Earth magic still affects surrounding terrain, with a smooth two-metre transition outside the tile. Adjacent friendly tiles inherit the adjoining elevation; a tile cannot join existing tiles with incompatible elevations. Overlapping outer blend skirts use order-independent smooth weights. Square occupancy tests include tile corners for trunks, rocks, landmarks and other buildings. Buildings must fit fully inside one friendly tile; partial overhangs and enemy-tile construction are rejected. Ordinary building overlap remains enforced.

Foundation health is separate from terrain resistance: physical damage can destroy the tile, releasing its terrain support. Repair restores its saved elevation. Destruction hides the masonry rather than leaving a misleading intact support. Machines follow the resulting ground on the existing half-second structure update. No gravity-driven collapse or rubble simulation is introduced. Tile health does not gradually weaken deformation protection.

Authoritative state uses the existing invention save/replication schema, preserving old saves. Foundation position/yaw validation rejects rotated or off-grid tile checkpoints. Terrain vertex cache, main mesh, ray targeting, scattered environment props, starting region and deformation shading all invalidate for support changes. Partial damage leaves support unchanged and does not invalidate terrain or trigger a rebake. The deformation texture refresh drains under its existing time budget. Procedural masonry reuses the established invention materials/geometries; no new asset dependency.
