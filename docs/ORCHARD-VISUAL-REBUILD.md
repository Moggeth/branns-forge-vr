# Orchard visual rebuild

The Orchard's terrain, water, ecology, collision and replay stay authoritative in the landscape systems. Claude Opus 5.5 on the personal `claude.ai` subscription authored the visual kit, revised it after real-game captures, and supplied the ground and water shaders. The editable Blender source, deterministic builder, GLBs, shader originals, manifest and visual handoff are under `assets/source-commissions/opus-landscape-rebuild-20260929/`; the runtime copies are under `godot/assets/orchard_kit/`. This art source is independent of player saves.

`orchard_ground.gd` binds the commissioned shader and a 320 × 320 m exposure texture. `landscape_runtime.gd` paints cut depth and raised soil from the same before/after heightfields used for terrain motion, replays that visual state on load and undo, and keeps weather/path masks. `landscape_water.gd` binds the commissioned river and pool shaders; its generated meshes include tangents for their normal maps. `orchard_visual_kit.gd` samples the registered river, height, slope and water fields to place bank stones, reeds, low cliffs and scree in chunked MultiMeshes. It does not generate a competing landscape. The old horizon's opaque apron is suppressed in the Orchard only; it crossed above carved terrain while the Forge Yard and Kitchen still need their own aprons.

For fixed in-game review, run:

```powershell
& .\tools\godot\Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64_console.exe --xr-mode off --audio-driver Dummy --path godot --script res://landscape_visual_review.gd --quit-after 1800 -- --world-builder-preview --landscape-seed=290929
```

The capture set in ignored `outputs/landscape-visual-review/` covers eye height and tactical views before, during and after Sunder, Raise Earth and Grow Forest. It waits for the hydrology worker before final captures; otherwise cliff/water dressing is missing. The operation and material checks are `orchard_visual_kit_checks.gd`, `orchard_exposure_checks.gd`, `landscape_water_checks.gd`, `landscape_spell_checks.gd` and `landscape_hydrology_checks.gd`, all run with the portable Godot in `tools/godot/`. Use the offline flags named in `docs/LIVING-LANDSCAPE.md` for full game checks; never run a capture against the player's save.

The first isolated Blender render looked reasonable but was a poor predictor of the actual Godot scene: the initial ground shader produced angular flat patches, the damp bank reflected the sky as a grey strip, and a fixed horizon apron covered both the creek and Sunder. A revised Opus shader plus real-game capture resolved those defects. The first cliff placements put 8–10 m modules on a roughly 4 m cut; the adapter now sizes the smaller authored module to the local wall relief. Always inspect both camera heights after art changes, and wait for derived terrain systems to settle. The river's revised shader is still visually streaky at some angles; it needs a further Opus review from the corrected in-game captures. The spell transformations also need the dedicated VFX package, including fracture, debris, dust, growth and atmosphere. The fixed harness exposes these unfinished areas rather than treating import success as visual acceptance.

The visual layer is intentionally replaceable. It receives a bounded, seeded `landscape_runtime.visual_event` payload with world-space path/bounds and paired before/target terrain samples; it cannot edit terrain truth or physics. That interface is the handoff for Opus-authored transformation effects and future quality tiers. Keep repeated dressing batched, lights unshadowed and scarce, and measure dual-view frames after integration; a fast static scene does not establish VR comfort.
