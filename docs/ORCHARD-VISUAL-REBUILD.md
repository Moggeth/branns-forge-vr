# Orchard visual rebuild — art and integration handoff

This is an active visual pass, not final art acceptance. The playable Orchard has one deterministic landscape model; visuals read it rather than defining another terrain. Claude Opus 5.5 owns the 3D art direction and authored meshes, Blender builders, shader/material colour and surface treatment, river look, transformation VFX, and atmosphere/lighting grade. Sol owns the seeded fields, height/collision, hydrology, ecology, save/replay and undo, deterministic placement and batching, import/integration, focused checks, real-game captures, and performance measurement. A visual change that needs new gameplay data must use the bounded `landscape_runtime.visual_event` contract; it cannot quietly change terrain truth, physics or saves.

The curated editable archive is [assets/source-commissions/opus-orchard-art-family-20260929/](../assets/source-commissions/opus-orchard-art-family-20260929/): Blender source and builders, shader originals, TreeKit patch, manifest, comparison samples and the [rev-4/4b](../assets/source-commissions/opus-orchard-art-family-20260929/HANDOFF.md), [rev-4c](../assets/source-commissions/opus-orchard-art-family-20260929/rev4c/HANDOFF.md) and [rev-4e](../assets/source-commissions/opus-orchard-art-family-20260929/rev4e/HANDOFF.md) handoffs. Runtime copies are in `godot/assets/orchard_kit/`, `godot/orchard_vfx/` and `godot/tree_kit.gd`; `godot/orchard_visual_kit.gd` is the Sol placement adapter. The archive is art source, not player runtime state. The visual kit keeps mesh names and pivots stable so seeded placement can survive an art rebuild.

`landscape_runtime.gd` owns the live model, transitions, collision and replay. `orchard_ground.gd` binds the ground shader to the authored/mutable path, litter, bank and disturbance mask, plus the exposure texture derived from before/after height. `landscape_water.gd` binds river and pool shaders to water geometry. `orchard_visual_kit.gd` samples river, height, slope and water fields and batches bank stones, reeds, cliffs and scree. `atmosphere_pipeline.gd` already invokes the Orchard grade once per application; the grade multiplies its inputs, so a second invocation would compound it. `orchard_spell_vfx.gd` consumes bounded visual events and quality budgets for temporary walls, lips, debris and dust. Five seeded TreeKit apple forms use a separate RNG stream; the trunk and low fruit limb remain stable, while the second reachable apple can shift because it is selected from the two lowest limb clusters. The old horizon apron is suppressed only in the Orchard; it crossed above the creek and Sunder floor, while other zones still use theirs.

## Real-game review and recovery

From the repository root, import changed GLBs before a preview or capture, then run the save-isolated real game review. This uses the desktop renderer with XR off; it does not start or stop the user's VR session.

```powershell
$engine = 'tools/godot/Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64_console.exe'
& $engine --headless --editor --import --xr-mode off --audio-driver Dummy --path godot --quit
& $engine --xr-mode off --audio-driver Dummy --path godot --script res://landscape_visual_review.gd --quit-after 1800 -- --world-builder-preview --landscape-seed=290929
```

Require `LANDSCAPE_VISUAL_REVIEW` with `failures: []`, `save_isolated: true`, seed `290929`, and 20 saved 1200 × 750 PNGs in ignored `outputs/landscape-visual-review/`. The ten stages cover natural brook/bank, before/during/after Sunder, before/during/after Raise, and before/during/after Grow, each from Hero eye and tactical cameras. The harness waits for hydrology to settle before final views. Compare matching views before and after each operation, especially `04-raise-before-*` against `06-raise-after-*`; a single after frame can misidentify existing terrain as a spell effect. Keep the previous capture set or copy it to an ignored comparison folder before rerunning, because the harness replaces its named PNGs. A script parse error can leave a graphical preview idling until its quit bound; check the first `SCRIPT ERROR` and the named result marker, not only exit code. `--check-only --script res://landscape_visual_review.gd` is a quick parse gate when source has changed.

The earlier isolated Opus preview was useful for iteration but not authoritative. Its first synthetic macro mask set alpha to zero, hiding broad authored disturbance that the real Orchard samples. Later disposable previews used the real mask, ground textures, TreeKit and grade, but still had stand-in terrain, river and operations. Always reimport after syncing changed GLBs into that preview because its sync clears imports. The original 60-turn Opus commission hit its turn cap; resuming the same session worked and preserved visual context. A new commission need not discard that context when a cap is reached.

## What the comparisons show

The initial real captures exposed chalk-white kit rocks, a washed river/near bank, repeated Sunder wall and lip rhythms, a broad brown Raise area, and near-identical apple crowns. Opus rebuilt the older kit with muted fractured stones, clustered bank lip modules and reeds; retuned the river and valley fog; broke Sunder walls into uneven runs with gaps and rockfalls; narrowed and interrupted the Raise tear; and added five seeded apple forms. Sol integrated the assets while keeping terrain, save and gameplay ownership in the landscape systems, and added deterministic reed-bed gaps. These are directional improvements seen in previews and some integrated captures, not blanket approval of the final scene.

The first rev-4 diagnosis of the brown Raise area was incomplete. The real `04-raise-before` view already shows a brown path where the mound will rise. At the Raise centre, local `(-43, 44)`, the authored mask has red path value `1.0`, blue bank `0`, and disturbance alpha `0`. Rev-4b suppressed alpha/slope soil on lifted turf, but left `m.r * 0.9` in the soil mix, so the existing path continued over the hill. Opus revised this against actual game captures three more times: rev-4c broke the road into conspicuous Voronoi paving, rev-4d replaced that with unwanted contour stripes, and rev-4e uses a restrained, broad fade with a few soft old-tread remnants. The latest game capture removes the obvious regular patterns and preserves the path outside the lift. The straight-looking boundary near the foreground and the subdued spell reveal still need art direction; this is a better interim treatment, not final acceptance. Rev-4d reached its turn cap after producing its shader and isolated preview, so the integrated rev-4e and its handoff are the complete current version.

Actual-game desktop captures for this revision (seed 290929, 1200 × 750): [river at Hero height](images/orchard-visual-review-20260929/00-brook-bank-natural-hero-eye.png), [settled Sunder from above](images/orchard-visual-review-20260929/03-sunder-after-tactical.png), [ground before Raise](images/orchard-visual-review-20260929/04-raise-before-hero-eye.png), [ground after Raise](images/orchard-visual-review-20260929/06-raise-after-hero-eye.png), and [settled Grow Forest at Hero height](images/orchard-visual-review-20260929/09-grow-after-hero-eye.png). These are evidence for review, not a claim that the headset view has been tested.

Hard triangular yellow/olive meadow tints remain visible in real and preview tactical views. Replacing the old `fwidth` fade, adding a tangent frame, removing terrain shadows in an A/B preview and inspecting the smooth grass texture did not eliminate them. The 512 × 512 mask's green and alpha channels have no large one-pixel steps at the sampled source (maximum adjacent deltas about 0.216 and 0.176), but this does not settle shader minification or slope/aspect thresholding. The next discriminating art diagnosis is the same seed and camera with temporary grayscale `ALBEDO` outputs for `m.g`, `m.a`, `slope_soft` and `soil_amt`; soften only the term that actually carries the triangles. This diagnostic should be done in an isolated shader/preview, then judged against the real game.

Other open critiques: Sunder lip caps were darkened and reshaped in rev-4c but still read as assembled green pieces at tactical height; cliffs can read as stacked slabs close up; Raise outcrops can float during animation; and the permanent apple trees are still static. Some ground, rock and spell details remain technical/prototype art rather than a finished natural landscape. Keep these limits visible in the next art brief rather than treating an import or a clean screenshot marker as visual acceptance.

The art commission used the personal `fauxgrady@gmail.com` Claude subscription (`authMethod: claude.ai`, Pro) in a cleaned child environment and explicit `claude-opus-5-5`. Session metadata reported that same model; no Anthropic API billing path was used. The initial isolated commission took 1,307.175 s, its same-session continuation 698.685 s, rev-4c 165.871 s, rev-4d 91.698 s and rev-4e 31.561 s. Rev-4d hit a turn cap after producing a shader/preview; rev-4e completed with a handoff, then Sol integrated and captured the actual game. The latest editor import after GLB changes took 3.347 s and the final 20-frame real-game capture took 13.773 s. These timings are retained in ignored `logs/work-timing.csv`; the source and decisions are in the curated archive above.

## Checks and performance boundary

Use focused offline Godot checks after each relevant change, with `--headless --xr-mode off --audio-driver Dummy --path godot --script res://NAME.gd`: `orchard_visual_kit_checks.gd` for deterministic placements/caps, `tree_kit_forms_checks.gd` for form silhouettes and six interactive apples, `orchard_exposure_checks.gd` for raised/cut surface state, `landscape_water_checks.gd` for water, `orchard_vfx_checks.gd` and `landscape_spell_checks.gd` for spell visuals/behavior, and `landscape_hydrology_checks.gd` for settled water. The reed check passed at seed 290929 with 295 reeds and 84 bank lips; the form check passed four seeds × five forms, with reachable apple centres 1.5–1.8 m high and at most 1.95 m horizontally from the trunk. The final save-isolated capture run recorded 20 PNGs and no failures. `node service/test.cjs` passed 7/7. The full `node service/integration.cjs` initially found that the expanded world sent more than the service's 100-target context limit, preventing Brann's hand-in. The production target serializer now sends a stable, origin-ranked subset; Brann's hand-in, generated weapon damage and adventure smoke then passed. The full integration suite still fails later collection/handoff/progression checks, which are outside this visual pass and should not be reported as green. A clean parse/import/check suite establishes integration, while paired captures establish visual evidence.

Measure dual-view cost only after art import and shader compilation have settled and Opus/other GPU users are idle. Use graphical `landscape_stress_check.gd` with `--world-builder-preview`, matching cameras, 24 guardians and the 30 fps/65% tactical view; compare `--visual-ablation=none|orchard-kit|horizon|spell-vfx` serially. Record wall and CPU p95, main/tactical GPU p95 when available, draws, primitives and spikes, then repeat when the scene changes.

| Empty, Hero only | 14.20 ms | — | 1.97 ms | — | 336 / — |
| Empty, dual view | 14.42 ms | — | 1.67 ms | 3.53 ms | 336 / 337 |
| 24 guardians, dual view | 16.79 ms | 34.35 ms | 1.96 ms | 8.88 ms | 399 / 505 |

These are short, sequential desktop proxy samples, not a controlled GPU experiment. The art kit and VFX visibly cost tactical GPU time, but neither ablation improved the measured whole-scene wall p95 in this run. One water/terrain update caused a ~34 ms maximum spike; that deserves VR scrutiny. Raw results are in ignored `outputs/landscape-visual-review/perf-*.json`. An earlier sample taken while SteamVR Environments and an Opus art process were active showed roughly 94 ms baseline p95 and 1,251 draws; it is contention evidence, not an isolated game regression. Desktop dual-view timings do not establish Vive Pro 2 stereo performance or comfort. Do not stop the user's SteamVR or live game just to get an idle benchmark.
