# Orchard environment library: handoff

Commission `work/opus-orchard-20260927`. This is 23 independent GLBs: 10 required pieces plus variants and LODs. All of it is generated by one deterministic Blender script. Nothing outside this folder was touched, and `input/` is unchanged.

## Rebuild (measured 2026-09-27)

```
B=../../tools/blender-4.5.13-windows-x64/blender.exe          # Blender 4.5.13 LTS
$B --factory-startup -b --python output/builder/build_orchard_library.py     # 23 GLB + .blend + 42 renders, ~6.6 s wall
$B --factory-startup -b --python output/builder/validate_glbs.py            # independent re-import, exit 1 on problems, ~3 s
python output/builder/contact_sheet.py                                      # Pillow; labelled sheet
python output/builder/make_manifest.py                                      # manifest.json
```
Add `-- --no-render` to the build to skip the Eevee renders. The build is **byte-deterministic**: two consecutive builds produced identical SHA-256 for every GLB. Each asset has a fixed seed in `CATALOGUE`. To make a new variant, add a catalogue row with a new seed. Don't edit the GLBs by hand.

## Files
- `glb/*.glb`: one self-contained asset per file (no external textures). Each file has a root node named after the asset, with one child mesh per role (`_Trunk`, `_Canopy`, `_Fruit`, `_Stone`...).
- `source/orchard_library.blend`: every asset in its own collection, laid out in a row for editing, plus the `RenderStage` and `ReviewScenes` collections.
- `builder/`: `build_orchard_library.py` (builder), `validate_glbs.py`, `contact_sheet.py`, `make_manifest.py`.
- `godot/orchard_post_import.gd` (import script) and `godot/orchard_finish.gd` (runtime helper). See Import below.
- `renders/`: `contact_sheet.png`, `<id>_front.png` and `<id>_threequarter.png` per asset (LOD1 shown for the mature tree only), `review_scale_lineup.png` (1.75 m blue player marker) and `review_tiling_joins.png` (4 walls, corner, end, and a 3-straight + curve path run).
- `manifest.json`: per asset: bounds, size, triangles, material slots, nodes, category, collision, LOD, joins/exit transforms, GLB bytes, re-import triangle count and renders. `build_report.json` and `validation.json` are the raw inputs.

## Import into Godot 4.7.2 (important)
All colour is in **COLOR_0 vertex colours**; every material has a white base colour. That keeps the library to 9 tiny materials that batch well. It also matches the ground kit, which already renders with `vertex_color_use_as_albedo` material overrides.

**Found during the disposable Godot 4.7.2 import test:** the importer only enabled *albedo from vertex colour* on the 2nd and later surfaces of a mesh. First surfaces imported **white** (38 of 45 surfaces failed the check). There are two verified fixes; pick one:
1. Set `godot/orchard_post_import.gd` as each GLB's *Import Script* (`import_script/path` in the `.glb.import` file). In a cold import of all 23 GLBs, every surface came out correct (`GODOT_CHECK bad=0`). Copy the script into the project **before** the first import. In the first attempt the files imported ahead of the script missed it, and a clean re-import fixed it.
2. At runtime, call `OrchardFinish.apply_tree(instance)` or `OrchardFinish.apply_mesh(mesh)` before building MultiMeshes. This was also verified with `bad=0`.

The GLB materials are back-face culled (`doubleSided=false`), except `orchard_blossom` (thin petals). Every other mesh is closed. `validate_glbs.py` checks winding using signed volume, and checks that paths face up.

## Placement guidance
- **Origins** sit at the ground-contact centre, with +Y up and the front facing +Z. Trees, rocks and walls sink 3–9 cm below y=0 so they seat on gentle slopes. Random yaw is safe for everything except the modular walls and paths.
- **Mature trees** have a 0.44 m trunk diameter at the base and a crown of about 4.7 m. The canopy underside stays at 2.05 m or higher for head clearance, so you can walk under the crown edge; the lowest apples hang around 2.0–2.4 m. For walkable orchard rows, space trees **6–7 m** apart, with 8 m between rows. Jitter by ±0.6 m, apply uniform scale 0.85–1.15, and mix `mature` / `mature_b` / `young` about 45/35/20, with autumn used as a zone or season swap. The young tree's canopy starts at 1.25 m, low enough to pick from at hand height.
- **Fruit** is decorative (`_Fruit` node). Hide that node wherever Godot spawns physical apples, so you don't get doubles.
- **Wind:** `_Canopy` is a separate mesh, so a vertex-shader sway can target only the leaves. Weight the sway by local height above about 2 m; there's no baked weight channel.
- **Hedges** are on a 3 m module. Place runs at 2.9–3.0 m spacing and yaw them to follow walls or field edges. Use them as a soft boundary at head height (1.5 m).
- **Flowering shrubs** (1.4 m) go in clusters of 2–4 along paths and wall bases. You can walk through them.
- **Rocks** use the existing kit's ranges: scale 0.72–1.32 and random yaw. `orchard_rock_mossy_small` also works as ground-kit filler, and `orchard_rubble_split` belongs at wall ends and field corners.
- **Walls** are on a 4 m module with the centreline at z=0. `straight` spans x −2..+2. `corner_broken` owns the corner block, and its arm ends are at (+2,0,0) and (0,0,+2), so a straight continues at (4,0,0) yaw 0 or at (0,0,4) yaw 90°. `end_broken` is full height at x=−2 and falls into rubble toward +X. The coursing is a random running bond, so you can repeat the same straight and the seam won't read. For variety you can rotate a straight 180°, since both faces are finished.
- **Paths** are 4 m long, about 1.6–1.8 m wide, and lie flat: the soil sits at y = +0.006 and flush stones top out at y ≤ 0.03. Put them on flat ground at y=0; there's nothing to trip over and no physics is needed. The edge outline is periodic over 4 m, so straights tile seamlessly. `curve30` exits at local (1.023, 0, 1.820) with yaw +30° (+Z turning toward +X), and `curve30_left` mirrors that (x −1.023, yaw −30°). Chain the next piece's entry, which is local (0,0,−2), to that exit. Three curves make a 90° turn. Turn cast_shadow off for paths.
- **Mountains:** `mountain_cliff_chunk` is an elongated mesa about 86 m wide × 56 m tall with one steep face (random azimuth per seed). Rotate and overlap two or three of them to build a ridge. `mountain_peak_snow` is 109 m tall. Place them 120–400 m out, at uniform scale 0.8–1.6, with yaw varied. The base skirt goes down to y=−4 to cover uneven terrain.

## Collision (none is built in; author proxies, as for the ground kit)
Trees: a capsule on the trunk (r 0.22, h 1.6 for mature; r 0.1, h 1.0 for young). The canopy has none. Stump: cylinder r 0.26 h 0.45. Hedge: box 3.0 × 1.35 × 1.1, centred at y 0.67. Shrub: none. Rocks: box or convex hull of the render mesh (320 tris is fine for convex). Rubble: one convex per split half, none for the shards. Walls: straight is box 4.0 × 1.3 × 0.62 at y 0.65; corner is two boxes along the arms (the collapsed notch is still waist height); end_broken is a full-height box over x −2..1.4 plus a sloped box for the rubble. Paths: none. Mountains: none (out of bounds) or a coarse box.

## Performance expectations (Mobile renderer / Quest-class budget)
- 9 materials total, all opaque. There are no textures, alpha, per-instance lights, animations or skinning. Everything is one mesh per role, so MultiMesh them per asset id as `GroundKit.populate` does.
- Mature tree: 6.7k tris at LOD0 and 1.5k at LOD1. Swap to LOD1 at about 35 m (young trees at 25 m), and hide the fruit beyond 20 m. Past about 140 m, cull or use distant billboards. A 300 m orchard of about 400 trees, with 40 at LOD0 near the player and the rest at LOD1, comes to roughly 0.85M tris before culling. Frustum culling in VR normally halves that. Set `visibility_range_end` on the MultiMesh instances.
- Walls are 2.8k tris per 4 m. A 100 m wall is about 70k; for long runs, set visibility ranges or use Godot's automatic mesh LOD (on by default at import).
- Mountains are about 1k tris each and always visible; that's cheap. Consider turning shadow casting off for mountains beyond the shadow distance.
- GLBs total 2.4 MB.

## Validation performed
- The builder ran cleanly. `validate_glbs.py` re-imported all 23 GLBs, each into its own empty Blender scene. It checked meshes, triangle counts, material names, COLOR_0 presence, ground-contact bounds, path flushness (−0.01..0.029 m) and winding. Result: `PROBLEMS []`.
- Disposable Godot 4.7.2 projects in `scratch/` (Mobile renderer, headless `--import` with a check script): all 23 load as PackedScenes. With the import script: `GODOT_CHECK bad=0`. With the runtime helper: `bad=0`.
- Determinism: two consecutive builds gave identical GLB SHA-256 hashes.
- Visual review of every render, the scale lineup and the tiling/join scene.

## Failed attempts and fixes (in order)
1. Path pebbles were rescaled by dividing by their top height, and near-flat pebbles produced a 7.9 m spike. Fixed with a min–max height remap. The validator now fails any path outside a y range of −0.02..0.04.
2. The flowering shrub floated 7 cm above the ground; the blob centre heights were fixed.
3. The first rocks looked like geodesic balls, because each tiny icosphere triangle got its own random tone. Fixed by cutting deeper planes and colouring per quantised facet normal. The stone was also too pale and the moss too lime, so both were darkened and desaturated, and the moss threshold was raised.
4. The mountains read as a "wedding cake". I added strata drift, a variable ledge depth per layer, erosion gullies and a steeper cliff face, and made the peak close properly.
5. LOD1 trees used a different random stream, so their skeleton didn't match LOD0. LOD1 now consumes the same stream and throws away the skipped geometry.
6. The review-scene duplicates inherited the hidden render flag and rendered empty, which was fixed.
7. Godot didn't apply vertex colour on first surfaces (see Import). Fixed with the import script and runtime helper, both verified.
8. With back-face culling switched on, the corner wall's Z arm rendered inside-out. Its transform was a swizzle, which mirrors the geometry; it's now a proper rotation. That prompted a signed-volume winding check, which then found **inverted apples** (the lathe ring order was clockwise); they were flipped. Everything passes now.

## Known limitations
- Canopies are smooth, opaque lobes: charming and cheap, but no leaf-level detail. Up close in VR they read as stylised clumps (this was intentional). There's no baked wind weight.
- Fruit placement doesn't coordinate with the game's `reach_points`. Treat it as decoration only.
- The trunk mesh is not the chop-band mesh from `tree_kit.gd`. If chopping needs these trees, keep tree_kit's physics and chop logic and use these meshes as visuals, or swap them to tree_kit on interaction.
- Path margins use the muted colour `7f7447`; they don't blend with the ground-kit grass material. Scatter ground-kit tufts along the edges to hide the seam.
- Walls are through-stone slabs with a hidden dark core. The stone faces are chamfered hulls, which gives a slight "brick" regularity up close.
- There's only one hedge module and no hedge corner piece; overlapping two hedges at 90° works acceptably.
- The renders use Blender Eevee with a studio sun, so the in-game look will differ with the zone lighting and any ground-kit style tint overrides.
- `scratch/` holds the disposable Godot check projects and hash files. It isn't a deliverable, and it's safe to delete.
