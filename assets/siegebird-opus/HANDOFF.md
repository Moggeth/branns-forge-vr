# Siegebird — "Kingfisher" (Opus commission) handoff

## Concept
Seen from above, the crossbow is a kingfisher in flight. The walnut body tapers into a fanned enamel-and-ivory tail. The prod is a pair of swept falcon wings: laminated horn limbs with a kingfisher-teal face, ivory edges and eleven layered primary feathers. A hooked brass beak sits under the bolt, and crystal eyes are set in the head. When the string is drawn the wings fold back and inward, like a stooping falcon.

Brass talons on the sled hold the string. The trigger is a talon. The crank knob ends in a little brass bird head; it counter-rotates, so the head always faces forward while you wind. The rear eyepiece is open and a crystal bead front sight is held on fine wires in a bronze swan-neck mount. The support hand holds a leather keel-bar ("breastbone") and the primary hand a raked leather pistol grip.

Palette: textured walnut and heartwood, brass, bronze, blued steel, horn, ivory, kingfisher-teal and ember enamel, oxblood leather, teal crystal, waxed cord.

## Moving parts
- Crank with the counter-rotating bird-head knob.
- Cone clutch: the male cone seats in a female cup on the pinion shaft with about 1 mm clearance, and slides 27 mm out toward the crank hub.
- 10-tooth involute pinion driving a 20-tooth, 5-spoke reduction gear.
- Drum with the crown ratchet.
- Latch pawl.
- Talon trigger.
- Sled: a bronze carriage wrapping the T-rail, with brass talon claws hooking over the string and a cable outrigger.
- Loaded bolt: ivory shaft, feather-vane fletching kept clear of the rail, and a steel "beak" head.

## Clearance verification (Blender builder)
The builder poses the model exactly as `mechanism()` does across 43 states:
- 33 winding states (draw 0→1, with the crank through two full turns),
- 9 release states (clutch out, latch swung),
- 1 state with the trigger fully squeezed.

In each state it tests every moving part against the static body, the wings, the optic and each other, using triangle-overlap BVH tests.

**Contact handling is zone-checked.** A cord touching another object is accepted only if every intersecting triangle has a vertex inside that object's intended contact zone for the current pose:
- Wings: the two tip nocks.
- Sled: the string claws and the cable anchor.
- Bolt: the nock.
- Body: the grooved cable-guide sheave only.
- Drum: the cable tangent and wind-on band.

Any other contact, including cable against the rest of the body or string against the body, is reported as unexpected. **Any unexpected result aborts export (exit 4), keeping the previous GLB.**

**Result: 0 unexpected intersections; 950 zone-valid contacts.**

This is triangle-surface testing on the selected parts at 43 sampled states. It does not prove there is no clipping between every pair or between samples, and it does not detect one part fully enclosed inside another. Static-vs-static geometry is not tested.

Clearances were designed in rather than exempted:
- The crowned rail head sits 0.8 mm under the sloping string.
- Sled hooks clear the rail by 1.3–1.7 mm.
- Wing feathers sit below the string plane and outside the crank's reach.
- The clutch clears the crank hub by 2 mm when released.

## Limitations
- **Gear phase in the uninitialised file:** in the raw game-contract GLB, all rotations are zero and the Reduction has not yet received the runtime's π/20 offset, so its teeth sit half a tooth out of phase with the pinion. Once the runtime applies its pose they mesh correctly (0 overlaps in the sweep). A showroom export posed through the runtime avoids this.
- **Clutch and guide are stand-ins for rotating parts.** The clutch is rotationally symmetric and does not spin, since the runtime never rotates it. The cable guide is a fixed grooved sheave, since the contract has no node to rotate it.
- **The clearance test has limited scope.** It is triangle-surface testing at sampled states only. It does not prove there is no containment or intersection between samples, and it does not model the player's tracked hands.
- **Not yet tested in-game.** VR hand comfort, the reel-socket fit and headset performance have not been checked in the actual game build.
- **Custom materials are replaced at runtime.** The runtime assigns a shader override (`siege_rope`) to the cords, and it adds its own reel socket, selector tab, lamp and label geometry.
- **Some procedural details are coarse up close.** The talon guide bracket is a simple wire form, and the wood grain is a planar projection, so seams are visible on end grain.
- **No gameplay was changed.** No files outside this folder were modified.
