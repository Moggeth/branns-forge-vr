title: A new look for the land: ground, water, trees and mountains
---
- **The ground is painted, not plain.** Seven new hand-tuned ground layers (lush grass, meadow, mossy forest floor, soil, sand, rock and snow) blend into each other by their own bumps and hollows, so grass gives way to soil between the clumps and snow settles in the dips first. No more stretched smears on cliffs, and no repeating tile pattern.
- **Mountains have bones.** Steep high slopes now show sharp ridges and soft gullies, with rock breaking through and snow lying in streaks down the gullies instead of round patches.
- **The far forest is the real forest.** Distant trees are now drawn from the very same tree models you chop up close, lit live by the sun and sky. Walk toward a wood and it doesn't swap into a different-looking tree. Far away, trees bow out one at a time into the haze instead of vanishing at a line.
- **No more terrain popping.** The land's detail now changes smoothly with distance, so hills no longer jump or reshape as you walk or ride.
- **Clear water.** Look into a lake or river and you'll see the bed darken with depth, sunlight ripples dancing on the shallows, and foam lapping at the shore. Rivers carry foam streaks downstream.
- **Air you can see through.** Far valleys fade into soft blue haze while the peaks above it stay crisp, so the world finally feels as big as it is. Hollows sit a touch darker and crests catch a little more sky.
> This is the first part of a shared art kit for every world. The Great Wilds, Expeditions and The Underways use it now; Brann's Valley keeps its current look for now.
