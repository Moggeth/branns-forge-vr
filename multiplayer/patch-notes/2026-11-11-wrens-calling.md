title: The Siegebird, remade: Wren's Calling
---
- **The Siegebird has been rebuilt by hand.** Same crossbow, same birds, same feel in your hands, but every part has been remodelled. The wings now have gilded quills and a lacquered sheen, and the stock carries the brass plate of its maker, Hollis Wren of the Dawnhold aviary.
- **Calling Stones.** Three sapphire stones are now set into the bow: one on each shoulder and one just under the sight, where you can see it while you aim. As you crank the winch, a sounding wire rings the stones one click at a time and they brighten. They flare when the string latches, and the shot drains them dark again. A full Siegebird glows softly in your hands, and at night it lights the wings around it.
- **Every bolt carries its bird's feather.** The bird that loads a bolt now fletches it with a feather in its own colours, so you can tell whose bird loaded each shot in flight. Other players' bolts show their birds' colours too.
- **A shot you can feel.** Firing now throws a blue flash, a ring of air, sparks and a puff of down off the wingtips, and the wings shiver with the recoil. Golden bolts burst in gold.
- **Bolts leave a trail.** Bolts now shed a faint trail of light and the odd drifting feather as they fly. Golden bolts trail glitter.
- **Bolts burst into feathers when they land.** Each hit now throws a double ring of light, a flash and a spray of feathers in the bird's colours, with sparks off stone and metal. Golden bolts burst into gold.
- **The stones wake when you draw.** Taking the Siegebird out makes the stones flare and the wings settle their feathers.
- Phones get the same stones, trails and bursts with fewer particles and simpler materials, so your frame rate stays where it was.
> Every effect comes from one light and two particle batches, however many bolts are in the air.
